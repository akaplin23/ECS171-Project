Does initialization still matter?

We retrain deep sigmoid networks on a heart failure dataset to test whether Glorot & Bengio's 2010 finding still holds: that how you initialize the weights decides whether a deep network trains at all.

Paper · Glorot & Bengio (2010), Understanding the difficulty of training deep feedforward neural networks  ·  Data · Heart Failure Prediction, 918 patients, 11 clinical features

02The finding

With the old initialization scheme, sigmoid networks stop training once they get deep — exactly what the paper predicts. Three of the six SGD runs collapsed into predicting one class for every patient.

Test AUC by depth, standard initialization (eq. 1). Crosses mark runs that collapsed — recall of exactly 0.000 or 1.000, i.e. one class for everyone.

Xavier initialization (eq. 16) with Adam, mean of 3 seeds. Flat across every depth — nothing collapses. The total spread is 0.0025 AUC, about twice the seed-to-seed noise.

What this means. Xavier did not produce a higher score — on this dataset nothing does, because 16 clinical features are close to linearly separable and logistic regression already reaches AUC 0.939. What Xavier bought was trainability: it worked at every depth we tried, while the old scheme failed outright past three layers. That is the paper's claim, reproduced.

04Data and method

Preprocessing

172 patients have Cholesterol = 0, which is not a measurement but a missing value — and 88% of them are sick, versus 48% of patients with a real reading. So we flag missingness as its own feature before imputing with the training median, otherwise the model learns that 237 mg/dl means heart disease. Skewed features get Box-Cox (Yeo-Johnson for Oldpeak, which goes negative), then standardization — which is what makes Glorot's equal-variance assumption hold in the first place.

Selection

Every choice was made on validation, never on test. Within a run, the best-validation-AUC epoch is checkpointed and early stopping ends it after 30 stale epochs. Across runs, a grid of 5 depths × 4 learning rates × 3 seeds — 60 runs — picked depth 4 at lr 3e-4. The decision threshold was scanned on validation afterwards. The 184 test rows were read once, at the end.
